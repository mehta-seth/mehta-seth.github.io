Something stuck in me while listening to Sultans Of Swing (A Night In London 1996). The keys-to-guitar transition at <a class="note-quiet" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yTcsgxU9jsA&list=RDyTcsgxU9jsA&start_radio=1&t=405s" target="_blank" rel="noopener">06:45</a> sounded sublime, like how I imagined London would feel. I was clearly pretty late to the party, but I strongly believe that great music comes to you, especially when you need it.

<div class="note-lyric">
{/* The two lines of the lyric, one per <p>, replacing the bracketed placeholders. */}
<p>Way on down south</p>
<p>Way on down south, London town</p>
</div>

Those lyrics got me curious about the story behind the song. Mark Knopfler was living with his brother in a first-floor council flat in Deptford. He heard a little dixie jazz band playing in a nearly empty pub, which inspired him to write the song. I was stoked to find that the first place I was going to call home in London, my student accommodation just off Old Kent Rd, wouldn't be far from there.

Months later, on a cold December evening, while walking along Shaftesbury Avenue, I saw a poster from 1967.

<div class="note-poster">
<Figure src={poster} alt="A 1967 newspaper advertisement headed Sundays at the Saville, a NEMS presentation, listing Pink Floyd with Tomorrow on 1 October, the Jimi Hendrix Experience on 8 October, Jr. Walker All Stars on 15 October, the Who on 22 October and the Cream on 29 October.">
<span class="fig-split"><span>Image: Pink Floyd</span><span>(very likely a copy)</span></span>
</Figure>
</div>

This was my entire playlist!

I had unknowingly passed by the iconic Saville Theatre many times, without really knowing its significance. It's a strange, somewhat powerful feeling realising that people you've admired from afar were once right there, separated from you only by time.

The road, once so important to London’s Rock ’n’ roll scene, was just a way for us to reach Tottenham Court Rd, and a particularly dark one.

I made this map to trace the history of this beautiful city of London, and so that I don't fail to recognise a landmark when I pass by another dimly lit alley again.
